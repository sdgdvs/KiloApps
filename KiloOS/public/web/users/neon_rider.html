01. Zero-Cycle Register Zeroing: XOR EAX, EAX vs MOV EAX, 0 1999-10-18 • 0xNEON
Every beginner uses MOV EAX, 0. This is a tragedy in opcode real estate. MOV EAX, 0 encodes to B8 00 00 00 00 (5 full bytes of machine code). Conversely, XOR EAX, EAX compiles to exactly 2 bytes: 31 C0.
; Bad (5 bytes): MOV EAX, 0 ; B8 00 00 00 00 ; God-Tier (2 bytes, zero latency on Pentium MMX): XOR EAX, EAX ; 31 C0
On Intel Pentium and Pentium Pro architectures, the internal register renaming hardware recognizes XOR r32, r32 as a dependency breaker. It eliminates false register dependencies without waiting for previous arithmetic results.
02. Fast Multiplication by 10 without MUL: The LEA & Shift Trick 1999-10-29 • 0xNEON
The MUL instruction on 486 and Pentium processors takes up to 10 to 42 clock cycles. In 3D rotation routines and rasterizers, multiplying by 10 or screen pitch (320) via MUL will crush your framerate. Instead, factor the multiplication into bitwise shifts and LEA (Load Effective Address):
; Multiplying EAX by 10 in 2 clock cycles: ; 10 = (8 + 2) = (EAX * 8) + (EAX * 2) MOV EBX, EAX ; Duplicate SHL EAX, 3 ; EAX = EAX * 8 SHL EBX, 1 ; EBX = EBX * 2 ADD EAX, EBX ; EAX = 8x + 2x = 10x!
Even cleaner in 32-bit protected mode with scale-index-base (SIB) addressing: LEA EAX, [EAX + EAX*4] (EAX * 5), followed by SHL EAX, 1 (EAX * 10). Total time: 2 clock cycles!
