Microsoft Announces Windows 2000 Will Be Shipped On 48,000 3.5-Inch Floppy Disks

"We heard our enterprise clients who refuse to install CD-ROM drives loud and clear," confirms VP of Optical Distrust.
BY TAD SCHOENFELD, SCALLION IT DESK • REDMOND, WA • DEC 29, 1999 • READ TIME: 3 MIN

In a stunning concession to corporate IT managers who still view optical laser discs as an "untested fad that summons static electricity," Microsoft Corporation announced Wednesday that the flagship release of Windows 2000 Professional will be distributed on forty-eight thousand 1.44-megabyte high-density 3.5-inch floppy disks.

The physical product will ship on a standard four-foot wooden freight pallet weighing approximately eight hundred and seventy pounds. According to Microsoft distribution director Greg Vender, standard automated installation is expected to take eighty-six consecutive hours of manual disk-swapping, provided Disk #27,419 does not suffer a catastrophic Cyclic Redundancy Check (CRC) error due to airborne biscuit crumbs.

"We understand that enterprise sysadmins pride themselves on physical endurance," Vender explained while demonstrating the reinforced steel pry-bar required to open Box 14. "When you insert Disk 38,912, that sense of artisan craftsmanship simply cannot be replicated by a five-minute CD-ROM spin-up."
