Linux 2.2.14 Kernel Released: SMP & IPChains Hardened

Linux
Posted by Rob 'CmdrTaco' Malda on Fri Oct 23 1999 @ 09:15AM
From the source-code-forever dept.

Linus Torvalds and Alan Cox have officially announced the release of Linux 2.2.14, bringing monumental stability enhancements to multi-processor (SMP) x86 rigs, complete packet filtering hardening in ipchains, and essential Y2K hardware clock rollovers.

Alan Cox writes: "This maintenance release squashes memory leaks in TCP socket teardowns under gigabit load and introduces reliable APIC irq routing for dual BP6 Celeron and Pentium III workstations. If you plan to leave your web and mail servers unattended on New Year's Eve, compiling 2.2.14 is considered mandatory."

Beowulf Cluster of 64 Celeron 300As Outperforms Cray Supercomputer

Hardware
Posted by Michael 'Hemos' Holve on Thu Oct 22 1999 @ 05:30PM
From the imagine-a-beowulf-cluster-of-these dept.

Researchers operating out of the Carlsbad Subterranean Acoustic & RF Testing facility have published astonishing LINPACK benchmarks: an interconnected array of 64 dual-socket ABIT BP6 motherboards populated with overclocked Intel Celeron 300A CPUs running at 450 MHz has clocked 24.8 GFLOPS sustained, dethroning several multimillion-dollar Cray Y-MP supercomputers.

The entire rig runs on Debian 2.1 (Slink) with Linux 2.2.13 SMP, networked via dual-ported 100Base-TX switches utilizing channel bonding. Chief engineer Dave 'Kernighan' states: "The total bill of materials was under $38,000 including 42U racks and heavy-duty box fans. We are using it to crunch acoustic wave equations in underground lithospheric strata."

Glide3 and Mesa 3.1 Unleash 3dfx Voodoo3 on XFree86

Gaming
Posted by Brian 'Zous' Shaler on Wed Oct 20 1999 @ 11:20AM
From the sixty-frames-per-second-in-tremor dept.

Gaming on Linux has made a titanic leap forward this week. The Open Source 3dfx Device Driver team, along with Brian Paul's Mesa 3.1 project, have rolled out rock-solid Glide3x drivers for 3dfx Voodoo2 and Voodoo3 accelerators under XFree86 3.3.5.

Early tests with Surreal Tournament and Tremor III Arena show framerates consistently exceeding 72 FPS in 1024x768 16-bit color. With Sound Blaster Live! and AWE64 ALSA modules working out of the box, is 1999 officially the year of the Linux gaming desktop?
